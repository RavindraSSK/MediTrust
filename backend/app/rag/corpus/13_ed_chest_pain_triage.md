The HEART score assigns 0 to 2 points each for History, ECG, Age, Risk factors and Troponin. Scores of 0 to 3 identify low-risk patients with roughly a 1 to 2 percent risk of a major adverse cardiac event within six weeks, scores of 4 to 6 intermediate risk (roughly 12 to 17 percent), and scores of 7 to 10 high risk (roughly 50 to 65 percent). The score is intended to support disposition decisions, not to replace clinical judgement.

The 2021 AHA/ACC chest pain guideline recommends obtaining an ECG within 10 minutes of arrival for suspected acute coronary syndrome, using high-sensitivity cardiac troponin as the preferred biomarker with serial 0/1-hour or 0/2-hour algorithms, and applying structured clinical decision pathways to identify patients who can be safely discharged and those who need admission or further testing.

Machine-learning risk estimates such as the MediTrust score are not validated substitutes for these pathways. They may help prioritise which cases a physician reviews first within a workflow, but final triage decisions rest with the clinical team and must integrate symptoms, vital signs, the ECG and troponin results.
