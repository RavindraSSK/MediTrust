Angina reproduced during exercise testing increases the post-test likelihood of significant coronary artery disease and is a component of the Duke Treadmill Score, where exercise-limiting angina carries the greatest weight. Exercise-induced chest pain that is accompanied by ST depression or a fall in blood pressure is a particularly high-risk combination that usually prompts further ischaemia testing or angiography.

Exercise capacity is one of the strongest predictors of mortality in both healthy people and people with cardiovascular disease. In a cohort of more than 6,000 men referred for exercise testing, each 1-MET increase in exercise capacity was associated with a 12 percent improvement in survival, and peak exercise capacity predicted death more strongly than established risk factors such as hypertension, smoking and diabetes.
