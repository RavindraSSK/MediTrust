The 2017 ACC/AHA guideline classifies adult blood pressure into four categories based on the average of two or more careful readings: normal (systolic below 120 mm Hg and diastolic below 80 mm Hg), elevated (systolic 120 to 129 mm Hg and diastolic below 80 mm Hg), stage 1 hypertension (systolic 130 to 139 mm Hg or diastolic 80 to 89 mm Hg) and stage 2 hypertension (systolic 140 mm Hg or higher or diastolic 90 mm Hg or higher). A resting systolic pressure of 140 mm Hg or more therefore falls in the stage 2 hypertension range.

Hypertension is one of the leading modifiable risk factors for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. The risk of coronary heart disease, stroke and heart failure rises progressively and continuously with systolic and diastolic pressure, and observational data cited by the guideline show cardiovascular mortality roughly doubling for every 20 mm Hg increase in systolic pressure above 115 mm Hg. Blood pressure lowering with lifestyle measures and, when indicated, medication reduces major cardiovascular events.

A single resting blood pressure recorded during a cardiac evaluation reflects one time point. The guideline recommends confirming a diagnosis of hypertension with repeated or out-of-office measurements and using the estimated 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk when deciding whether stage 1 hypertension warrants drug therapy.
