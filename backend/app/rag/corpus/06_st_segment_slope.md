The slope of the ST segment at peak exercise modifies the interpretation of ST depression. Horizontal (flat) or downsloping ST depression is considerably more specific for myocardial ischaemia than upsloping depression, and downsloping depression has the strongest association with multivessel coronary disease and adverse outcomes. Upsloping ST depression is frequently seen in healthy individuals at high heart rates and, on its own, is a weak marker of disease.

The MediTrust model receives the ST slope as recorded in the Cleveland data set (upsloping, flat or downsloping). In that cohort flat and downsloping slopes accompanied obstructive coronary disease far more often than upsloping slopes, which is consistent with the exercise testing guideline and explains why the slope carries substantial weight in the model.
