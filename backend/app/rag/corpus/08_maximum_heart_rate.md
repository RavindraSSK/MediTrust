Age-predicted maximal heart rate is commonly estimated as 220 minus age, or with the Tanaka formula 208 minus 0.7 times age. Failure to reach at least 85 percent of the age-predicted maximum during a symptom-limited exercise test, in the absence of rate-slowing drugs such as beta-blockers, is termed chronotropic incompetence.

Chronotropic incompetence and a slow heart-rate recovery after exercise (a fall of 12 beats per minute or less in the first minute) are each associated with increased all-cause mortality and with the presence of coronary artery disease, independent of ST-segment changes. A lower maximum heart rate achieved during testing therefore signals worse prognosis in the Cleveland cohort and in later validation studies.

A low peak heart rate can also result from beta-blocker therapy, deconditioning, early termination of the test or poor effort, so the value should be interpreted together with the reason the test was stopped and the patient's medication list.
