The risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease increases steeply with age and, at any given age, is higher in men than in premenopausal women. The ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Equations estimate 10-year risk of a first hard cardiovascular event from age, sex, race, total and HDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, blood pressure treatment, diabetes and smoking status, and age is the single most influential term in these and every other validated risk score.

Women more often present with symptoms other than classic exertional chest pain and are more likely to have non-obstructive coronary disease or coronary microvascular dysfunction. The 2021 chest pain guideline emphasises that women's chest pain must be evaluated as seriously as men's and that sex-specific presentation differences should not delay testing. In the Cleveland cohort male sex was strongly associated with obstructive disease, which is why the model assigns weight to sex.
