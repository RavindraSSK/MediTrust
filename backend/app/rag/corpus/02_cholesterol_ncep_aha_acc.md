The National Cholesterol Education Program Adult Treatment Panel III classification labels a total cholesterol below 200 mg/dL as desirable, 200 to 239 mg/dL as borderline high and 240 mg/dL or above as high. Elevated total cholesterol reflects a higher burden of atherogenic lipoproteins that contribute to plaque formation in coronary arteries.

The 2018 AHA/ACC multisociety cholesterol guideline treats LDL cholesterol as the primary atherogenic lipid. It recommends high-intensity statin therapy for adults with an LDL cholesterol of 190 mg/dL or higher, moderate-intensity statin therapy for adults aged 40 to 75 with diabetes, and, for adults aged 40 to 75 with LDL cholesterol of 70 to 189 mg/dL, a decision guided by the 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk estimated with the Pooled Cohort Equations together with risk-enhancing factors.

Total cholesterol is an input to the Pooled Cohort Equations, so higher values raise the estimated 10-year risk. Extreme values, for example above 400 mg/dL, may indicate a familial lipid disorder, a secondary cause or a measurement problem and warrant repeat testing with a full lipid panel before treatment decisions are made.
