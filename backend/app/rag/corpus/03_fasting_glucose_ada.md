The American Diabetes Association defines a fasting plasma glucose below 100 mg/dL as normal, 100 to 125 mg/dL as impaired fasting glucose (prediabetes) and 126 mg/dL or higher, confirmed on repeat testing, as diabetes. A haemoglobin A1C of 6.5 percent or higher is an alternative diagnostic criterion.

Diabetes is a major cardiovascular risk enhancer: adults with diabetes have roughly twice the risk of coronary heart disease and cardiovascular death of adults without diabetes. Guidelines from the American Diabetes Association and the American Heart Association therefore recommend intensive management of blood pressure and lipids in people with diabetes, including moderate-intensity statin therapy for most adults aged 40 to 75.

The Cleveland Heart Disease data set records only a binary flag for fasting blood sugar above 120 mg/dL. That historical cut-point lies within the modern prediabetes range, so a positive flag identifies a patient with at least impaired fasting glucose and should prompt confirmatory glucose or A1C testing rather than being treated as a diagnosis of diabetes on its own.
