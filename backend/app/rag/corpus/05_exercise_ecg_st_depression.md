The standard electrocardiographic criterion for a positive exercise test is 1 mm (0.1 mV) or more of horizontal or downsloping ST-segment depression measured 60 to 80 milliseconds after the J point. Upsloping ST depression is a less specific finding unless it is pronounced. The Cleveland variable "oldpeak" records the exercise-induced ST depression in millimetres relative to rest.

The magnitude and behaviour of ST depression relate to the ischaemic burden: depression of 2 mm or more, onset at a low workload or heart rate, depression persisting more than five minutes into recovery, or changes in multiple leads indicate more severe coronary disease and a worse prognosis. Pooled analyses report an exercise ECG sensitivity of roughly 68 percent and specificity of roughly 77 percent for obstructive coronary disease, so the test is interpreted together with symptoms and pre-test probability.

The Duke Treadmill Score combines exercise duration in minutes on the Bruce protocol minus five times the maximal ST deviation in millimetres minus four times the angina index (0 for no angina, 1 for non-limiting angina and 2 for exercise-limiting angina). Scores of +5 or higher indicate low risk, scores from -10 to +4 intermediate risk and scores of -11 or lower high risk, with four-year survival of approximately 99, 95 and 79 percent respectively in the original Duke cohort.
